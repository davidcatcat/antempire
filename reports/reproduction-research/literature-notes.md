Inferences (ours, not the authors'):
- The non-reproductive stage durations are differences of cohort means: 23.68 − 21.13 = 2.55 and 28.73 − 23.68 = 5.05, with different n at each milestone. They are not per-individual durations.
- Reproductive pupation 25.29 = 21.13 + 4.16 and eclosion 37.60 = 25.29 + 12.31, so these are composed values.
  - The SD 2.74 is not reproduced by adding independent variances (√(2.57² + 0.37²) = 2.60).
  - Male and gyne are pooled everywhere. No per-individual reproductive data are in the supplement.
- 255→227→208→194→93→80→52 are the S2 milestone counts. The 194→93 drop is not explained in the paper.
- Pupation spans days 19–27 (difference 8 d; "nine-day window" counts both ends).
- Longevity (S3 recomputed): minors 104→0 by day 98, soldiers 14→0. The estimator reproduces 26.25 / 56.00 / 29.78 d.

## 3. Reimer, Glancey & Beardsley 1991 (J. Econ. Entomol. 84:56–60) — full text (scan, OCR)

- Abstract: "Fenoxycarb-treated colonies shifted caste differentiation from workers to males at 3 wk after treatment. At 5 wk, 90% of larvae were male; the remaining 10% developed into intercastes."
- p.58: "produced male larvae at 2 wk after treatment… At 5 wk after treatment, 90% of the larvae were males; the remaining larvae developed into intercastes between soldiers and males."
  - Onset is "3 wk" in the abstract and "2 wk" in the body.
  - "Intercastes between soldiers and males" is the authors' wording. We do not interpret it biologically.
- p.58 controls: "No male, queen, or intercaste larvae were produced in control colonies; only worker and soldier larvae were produced."
- p.59: "never produced alate females. Instead, P. megacephala queens laid unfertilized (male) eggs after ingesting fenoxycarb."
  - Field (citing Reimer & Beardsley 1990): "96.1% of the pupae in fenoxycarb-treated plots were male… compared with 100% worker pupae in control plots".
  - Queens "stopped laying male eggs at 13 wk".
- Setup: "Thirty-six bigheaded ant colonies were collected from pineapple stumps in a ratoon field at Kunia, Oahu"; standardized to "1 queen, 25-50 eggs, 1-2 ml brood, 200-400 workers, and 20-40 soldiers"; 25 °C; ~10 h photophase.
- Pyriproxyfen stopped oviposition by week 6; "Some of these queens lived for 5 to 6 mo."

## 4. Fournier, Tindo, Kenne, Mbenoun Masse, Van Bossche, De Coninck & Aron 2012 (R101/R110) — full text read

- Sampling: "Sixty-four nests… from eight populations in Cameroon in February 2009": 35 urban (Yaoundé) and 29 rainforest (Campo area).
- Clades: "P. megacephala var. 1 corresponding to the specimens collected in urban areas and P. megacephala var. 2 to those sampled in rainforest". "Urban populations belong to the same phylogenetic group than those introduced in Australia" (COI only).
- Supercolonies: "the eight populations sampled make up four mutually aggressive supercolonies. The maximum distance between nests from the same supercolony was 49 km and the closest distance between two nests belonging to two different supercolonies was 46 m."
  - Aggression in YU (two supercolonies): 46.12% of trials.
- Queens: "revealed a low degree of polygyny and corresponds to groups with 1–4 singly-mated queens. This is largely below the number of queens observed in the field (up to 71 queens were collected from a single nest of the population YS)." No other queen counts.
- Mating frequency is **not measured**: "The assumptions of single mating and equal partitioning of reproduction among queens are corroborated by a previous study [34]" (= Fournier et al. 2009, R113, Australia).
- Queen–mate relatedness 0.694 ± 0.038 ("inferred from the sperm stored in queen's spermatheca"). Queen–queen relatedness 0.634 ± 0.075 (Results) vs 0.637 (Discussion).
- Inbreeding: f per supercolony 0.207–0.431, "approximately 50 to 75% full-sib mating".
- Diploid males: "no such diploid males were found in our sample (N = 88, unpublished data)".
- Interpretation: "new queens are recruited among daughters"; "mating takes place in the nests between related individuals"; "the main mode of colony reproduction occurs via dependent foundation, whereby a queen and a few workers leave the natal nests to start a new colony nearby" (inferred from genetic × geographic distance correlation).
- The words "nuptial flight", "budding" and "alate" do not occur in the text.

## 5. Liu, Tseng, Tatsuta, Tsuji, Tay, Singham, Yang & Neoh 2022 (R141) — full text read

- "A total of 30 P. megacephala colonies were sampled from urban parks in four Taiwanese regions (six parks in Taipei [TP], eight in Taichung [TC], eight in Kaohsiung [KH], and eight in Hualien and Taitung [HT]…) during 2018–2019."
  - Also 6 Okinawa and 13 Hawaii colonies. "All samples used in this study were minor workers." 8 workers per colony.
- "Our preliminary study revealed that colonies located more than 100 m apart acted aggressively." No method or data given.
- Haplotypes: TW1 77% (resembles Hawaii/Okinawa), TW3 20% (same group as USA, Australia, Cameroon, Mauritius, Caribbean), TW2 3% (12.5% in Discussion).
  - "Our preliminary result indicated no cryptic species in our sampling areas (Liu, 2020; Appendix S2)".
  - Inconsistency: the Fig. 6 caption says "mitochondrial control region", the Methods say COI.
- Long-distance "human-assisted jump dispersal". No isolation by distance.
- Nothing on queens, males, flights, budding or mating in Taiwan.

## 6. Sarnat, Fischer, Guénard & Economo 2015 (R99) — full text read

- "colonies are polygynous, and dependently founded via budding, with nests in large areas often forming supercolonies (Hoffmann 1998)".
- Table 2 row: polygynous [16 = Hölldobler & Wilson 1990]; unicolonial [17 = Hoffmann 1998]; dependent [17].
- No statement on nuptial flights, males or gynes for *P. megacephala*.

## 7. UF/IFAS EDIS IN712 (Warner & Scheffrahn; R95) — full page read

- "Although Vanderwoude et al. (2000) report that in Australia dispersal of new colonies is through budding without a nuptial flight, in south Florida nuptial flights of alates (winged reproductives) can be observed during the winter and spring (Scheffrahn, unpublished observations). Fertilized queens shed their wings and find a nest site where they will begin laying eggs."
- "Colonies can have large numbers of fertile queens (Wilson 2003)".
- The page also repeats GISD numbers (lifespan 78 d / 38 d), which the repo already excludes (#38 §0).
- The Vanderwoude 2000 abstract (Austral Ecology 25:253) is about community impact only. **The budding statement attributed to it is unverified** (full text not accessible: HTTP 403).

## 8. Other sources

- **Reimer & González-Hernández 1993 (R138)**, abstract only.
  - Earlier work showed the ant "can infest ant-free pineapple fields by budding" but "found no evidence for haplometrosis".
  - In the lab "Solitary queens began to lay eggs 1–2 days after setup".
  - "these laboratory results have not been verified in the field".
- **Sameshima, Miura & Matsumoto 2004 (R142)**, abstract only (closed access).
  - "all three castes have mesothoracic wing discs, at least in their early stage of the final larval instar".
  - Minor discs "disappear during the prepupal stage"; major discs "evaginate… subsequently degenerate by apoptotic cell death".
  - Colony origin is not in the abstract.
- **Hoffmann 1998** (Pacific Conservation Biology 4:250, doi 10.1071/PC980250), abstract only. Sarnat's primary source for budding and unicoloniality; the founding text was not seen.
- **Fournier, de Biseau & Aron 2009 (R113)**, abstract only. Australia: no aggression up to 3000 km; the single-mating assumption of Fournier 2012 comes from here.
- **Wills et al. 2014 (R97)**: introduced populations sampled (Australia, Hawaii, Mauritius, Florida, South Africa) fall in one clade.
- **Salata & Fisher 2022** (PeerJ 10:e13263), full text grepped. Malagasy revision of the megacephala group; nothing on reproduction.
- **San Juan, Azémar & Dejean 2025 (R100)**: "future queens mating without nuptial flight so that the colonies reproduce through budding" is a generic statement about imported ants, not *P. megacephala* data.
- **Not found** (searched; only non-citable forum/blog hits): any peer-reviewed *P. megacephala* field study of nuptial flights, mating location, sex ratio, male production season, male longevity or sperm storage, from Hawaii, Okinawa, Taiwan, Australia, South Africa, Mauritius or Galápagos.
